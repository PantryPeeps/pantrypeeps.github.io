Facts, research, and honest conversations about hunger, dignified support, and neighbors’ everyday realities.

What Is Food Insecurity?

Food insecurity is the limited or uncertain availability of nutritionally adequate and safe foods, or limited or uncertain ability to acquire acceptable foods in socially acceptable ways.

In plain language: a household can’t always count on access to safe food that provides needed nutrients. “Safe” means food is not spoiled or contaminated and has been handled and stored safely. “Nutrient-dense” foods provide nutrients such as protein, fiber, vitamins, and minerals. Variety matters: no single food provides everything, and every meal does not have to be perfectly balanced.

Some foods and drinks provide energy but relatively few nutrients. People sometimes call these “empty calories.” That phrase does not mean a food has no value or should never be eaten. The concern is whether a household can get enough variety of safe foods that are also nutrient dense over time.

A household’s choices are shaped by its budget, food prices, what nearby stores offer, distance, transportation, and time. When safe, nourishing options are costly or difficult to reach, food choices and variety may shrink.

Food insecurity can also disrupt eating patterns. This can mean eating smaller meals, skipping meals, or eating less than a person needs because there is not enough money for food. In the most severe cases, someone may be hungry but still go without eating.
