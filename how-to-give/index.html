Seasonal & Emergency Readiness

Year-Round Community Support

Hunger and hardship don’t follow a single calendar date. Planning ahead for holidays, school breaks, back-to-school transitions, and sudden emergencies helps keep local shelves stocked when families need them most, using care kits that are practical, respectful, and easy to carry.

Holidays & School Breaks

Kids lose access to daily school breakfasts and lunches during summer, winter, and spring breaks. Stock kid-friendly snacks, easy lunches, and festive meal staples ahead of time.

Back-to-School Needs

Pair shelf-stable lunchbox favorites with basic notebooks, pencils, hygiene items, and socks so families don’t have to choose between groceries and school supplies.

Displaced Neighbor Care Kits

Assemble lightweight, dignified grab-and-go kits with pop-top ready-to-eat meals, bottled water, wipes, socks, and travel toiletries for neighbors navigating fires, evictions, sudden displacement, or living without a fixed address.

Little Free Pantry Food-Safety Rule

Little Free Pantries should never be used for fresh, hot, leftover, or home-prepared food. Always stick to fully labeled, sealed, commercially packaged, shelf-stable items. Keep original labels intact on cans and keep inner bags inside their outer boxes (like cereal) so neighbors always know what is inside, ingredients, and allergen info.

FOLLOW EACH PANTRY’S POSTED STEWARD GUIDANCE AND FOOD-SAFETY RULES.

All donated items should be in date (within their best by, sell by, or use by date). Please check for popped, dented, or rusty cans and water damage on dry boxes. As a general guide, if a store would not be able to sell it currently, we kindly ask that you do not donate that item.

Could food still be safe past its sell-by or use-by date? Sure, but what these pantries offer is a sign from neighbors that everyone is worthy of fresh, in-date food, the exact same food you or anyone else could walk into a store and buy on the spot. A hand out should always be a hand up.
